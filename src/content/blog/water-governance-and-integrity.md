I’ve spent thirty years inside water governance — as Deputy Director General at South Africa’s Department of Water Affairs and Forestry, as Executive Director of the Water Integrity Network (WIN), and advising governments and river basin authorities across the SADC region and globally.

I know intimately what it looks like when institutions get water governance right. And I have seen, at close range, the human and economic cost when they get it wrong.

### The Illusion of the Flawless Organogram

Public institutions facing crises frequently turn to structural redesign:
1. They redraw the organogram.
2. They rename directorates.
3. They draft shiny new policy documents and procedural manuals.

Yet, within eighteen months, the same bottlenecks, vulnerabilities to corruption, and employee disengagement reappear. Why?

Because **institutions are made of people**. You cannot re-engineer water allocation, licensing, or infrastructure finance on paper without addressing the institutional culture, the tacit incentives, and the daily pressures faced by the officials responsible for executing policy.

### Grounding Integrity in Real Systems

Through Red Thread, our consulting work focuses on the practical architecture of integrity:
- **Anti-corruption and integrity frameworks** that protect both the institution and honest civil servants.
- **Water tenure and rights mechanisms** that safeguard vulnerable rural communities and ensure equitable allocation.
- **Transitional support** for merging or reforming water utilities and catchment management agencies.

This isn't theory brought in from outside consultants who leave behind thick binders. It is rooted in three decades of having sat on the other side of the table — having had to draft national legislation, defend regulatory choices, and manage complex multi-stakeholder negotiations under scrutiny.

Integrity is not an abstract virtue; it is a set of daily operational habits.
