Water is humanity's most essential common good, yet water governance is fraught with systemic vulnerabilities: institutional inertia, political interference, corruption risks, and climate unpredictability. Effective water sector transformation requires both strategic policy vision and an intimate understanding of how public bureaucracies actually operate.

Barbara Schreiner brings three decades of frontline senior leadership: former Deputy Director General in South Africa's Department of Water Affairs and Forestry (DWAF), Executive Director of the Water Integrity Network (WIN, Berlin), member of the South African National Anti-Corruption Advisory Committee, and advisor to the World Bank, OECD, and GIZ.

---

## Areas of Advisory Practice

### 1. Integrity Frameworks & Anti-Corruption
Designing and implementing robust integrity management systems for public water utilities, river basin organizations, and municipal authorities:
- Integrity risk assessments across procurement, licensing, and infrastructure contracting
- Whistleblower protection and internal accountability mechanisms
- Capacity building on corruption prevention for senior civil servants and board members

### 2. Institutional Reform & Agency Restructuring
Steering public bodies through complex institutional evolutions:
- Transition management for Catchment Management Agencies (CMAs) and regional water boards
- Organizational mandate reviews, alignment of statutory powers, and change management
- Multi-stakeholder participatory governance models bridging civil society, industry, and government

### 3. Policy & Legislative Drafting
Translating high-level political commitments into enforceable statutory provisions and practical operating procedures:
- Equitable water resource allocation mechanisms and pro-poor tariff models
- Drafting regulations, white papers, and national water strategies
- Transboundary water governance agreements and regional coordination frameworks

## Global Experience

Barbara's consulting practice bridges African local and national institutions with global multilateral platforms:
- **South Africa**: Drafting transformative post-apartheid water legislation, pricing strategies, and national water resource strategies.
- **Sub-Saharan Africa**: Advisory assignments across Kenya, Uganda, Zimbabwe, Zambia, and SADC transboundary river basins.
- **Multilateral Engagement**: Advisory roles with the OECD Water Governance Initiative, Open Government Partnership (OGP), and the Global Water Partnership.
